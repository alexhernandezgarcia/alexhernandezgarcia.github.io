#### Brief academic bio
I am an assistant professor at the Université de Montréal at [DIRO](https://diro.umontreal.ca) and a core academic member at [Mila](https://mila.quebec), the Quebec Artificial Intelligence Institute, in [Tiohtià:ke](https://www.concordia.ca/indigenous/resources/territorial-acknowledgement.html) / Montréal. Previously, I was a postdoctoral researcher working with [Prof. Yoshua Bengio](https://yoshuabengio.org/) and [Prof. David Rolnick](https://davidrolnick.com/). I defended my [PhD thesis]({{ site.url }}/thesis.pdf) in 2020 at the University of Osnabrück, which I started in 2016 with [Prof. Peter König](https://portal.ikw.uni-osnabrueck.de/~NBP/PeterKoenig.html), as a fellow of the Marie Sklodowska-Curie ITN [NexGenVis](https://www.nextgenvis.eu). I obtained my bachelor's and master's degrees at the [University Carlos III of Madrid](https://www.uc3m.es/Home). During my doctoral stuedies, I did research internships at the University of Cambridge with [Dr. Tim Kietzmann](http://www.timkietzmann.de/) and at the Spinoza Center for Neuroimaging with [Dr. Serge Dumoulin](http://www.spinozacentre.nl/dumoulin/).

#### Current research
My current work focuses on **machine learning research that can facilitate scientific discoveries to tackle the climate crisis and health challenges**. For example, I have ongoing collaborations on projects for antibiotics discovery, the design of novel battery materials and climate modelling with machine learning. In terms of methods, I work on developing generative models, such as [GFlowNets](https://github.com/alexhernandezgarcia/gflownet), and [active learning](https://github.com/milaforscience/activelearning/) for scientific discoveries. In particular, I am working on multi-fidelity active learning methods, crystal structure generation, molecular design and molecular conformation sampling. Furthermore, I am increasingly interested in critically analysing the societal impacts of so-called artificial intelligence. My previous research has studied the carbon emissions of machine learning models, the implications of big tech's influence in AI research and the role of AI researchers in the development of weapons systems.

#### My vision of science
I am a big fan of interdisciplinary research. I find it not only fruitful but also very fun to collaborate with researchers from other disciplines. I work on machine learning research that can be useful for other scientists, I try to establish collaborations with them and this often inspires new research directions.

Besides my research, I am a strong proponent of open science in the broadest sense, and I try to contribute to it with my actions and by getting involved in discussions about how to make science more inclusive, equitable, open, reproducible, transparent and environmentally conscious. One way I channel and feed these ideas is by being active in the community of [Science for the People](https://sftp-canada.org/). I often participate as mentor in initiatives to support students from under-represented groups, such as the [AI4Good Lab](https://www.ai4goodlab.com/), the [Fatima Fellowship](https://www.fatimafellowship.com/home) or LatinX in AI. At Mila, I have been a member of the Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Committee and the Sustainability Committee. With amazing colleagues, I co-organise the [Critical Science reading group](https://criticalscience.github.io) at Mila. In 2021/2022, I was the elected postdoctoral Mila Lab Representative. At NeurIPS 2021, I co-organised a [workshop on pre-registration in machine learning](https://preregister.science/) and in 2022 the [AI Helps Ukraine - Charity Conference](https://aihelpsukraine.cc/).
